Getting around

Your sidebar, the three places you'll use, and how to move around.

1 Your sidebar — three places

When you sign in, the menu on the left is short and focused — the three places you'll use, all the time. Wherever you are, they're one click away.

(the page you're on opens here)
  1. 1Styles — the full catalogue. Browse or search any style and open its data. This is also where the logo takes you.
  2. 2My tasks — your personal to-do list: everything that's waiting on you, sorted into buckets. Covered in guide 2.
  3. 3Reviews — the shared queue: every style currently awaiting review, regardless of who's on it. Covered in guide 3.

The logo at the top-left also takes you back to Styles at any time.

2 Which place, for what

A quick way to decide where to go.

Go to……when you want to
StylesLook something up. Find a specific style by name, customer, PO number or status — whether or not it's up for review — and open its data, care instructions and barcodes.
My tasksSee what's on you right now: reviews you've started and haven't finished, fresh documents waiting for a first look, and rejections you raised. Start here each day.
ReviewsPick up the next piece of work. The whole queue of styles awaiting review, longest-waiting first — grab one even if nobody has touched it yet.

3 What you focus on

You judge the finished documents

Your job is to decide whether each finished document is correct and safe to print. You don't change the data or re-make a document from here — if something's wrong or missing, you reject it with a note. It then gets fixed and re-run, and the corrected version comes back to you. (Full detail in guide 5.)

4 Signing in

You'll land on Styles when you sign in. Your name appears on any review you start — that's how teammates can see a style is already in good hands — and any half-finished review comes back to wait for you on My tasks.

Tip

Bookmark My tasks. It's the one page that answers “what should I do next?” — and it's where half-finished reviews come back to wait for you so nothing gets forgotten.
